Headline numbers

    Where dogs come from

    About half of Korean dog owners got their dog from someone they know, mostly for free. Pet shops come second, and they grew between the two surveys.

    The acquaintance route

    “From an acquaintance” is not one thing. It covers litters from a friend’s dog, animals that an owner could no longer keep, and people met online. People who take a pet this way say they trust the source and want to see the animal first.

    Home litters

    Most home litters are passed on to people the owner knows, and usually for free. These are small samples, so read them as a rough picture.

    Neutering

    Neutering rose between 2022 and 2023, most for dogs tied up outdoors. Fewer owners named fear of pain or cost as a reason not to neuter.

    Price

    Buyers paid the most at pet shops and the least when buying from an acquaintance.

    Reading the numbers

    This part is our interpretation, not a survey result.

    Acquaintance transfers are the largest single supply route for dogs (47.7% to 51.2%). Puppies pass on with no seller, screening or education step, so owner-side measures, such as neutering access and guidance that reaches people who receive a puppy as a gift, fit this route better than rules about sales. Pet shops (31.0%) and individual breeders (7.1%) both grew in 2025, so a market-side lever probably still matters.

    Data notes and open questions

    • Both ministry surveys are online panels of adults aged 20–64. The tables do not print how many owners answered. Dog owners under one year old work out to about 48 people (inferred from the percentages), too few to use.
    • “From an acquaintance” mixes home litters, takeovers and distant online acquaintances. The surveys do not say whether litters were planned.
    • Not asked in any source found: why givers give puppies away free, how many litters are accidental, and how disguised commercial sellers classify themselves (likely “individual breeder” or “paid from acquaintance”; interpreted).
    • Some individual breeders may be unlicensed commercial sellers presented as home breeders. Journalists report this, but no one has measured how many.
    • Awareness of the Animal Protection Act: 75.4% said they knew it in 2024 and 74.9% in 2025.
    • Next step: short interviews with owners who rehomed a litter would answer the “why free” question directly.
